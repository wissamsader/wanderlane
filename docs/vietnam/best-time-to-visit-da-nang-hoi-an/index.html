Best Time to Visit Da Nang & Hoi An

February–May is the sweet spot — dry season, before the real heat sets in — plus the honest trade-offs of a hot summer and Hoi An's autumn flood risk.

Da Nang and Hoi An share one climate, and it splits cleanly into three stretches: a dry, comfortable window, a hot-but-still-dry one, and a wet season with a specific flood risk worth planning around rather than glossing over. The short version: February–May is the sweet spot.

Why February–May

This is the region's dry season before the summer heat and humidity really set in, which makes it the most reliable stretch for both beach time in Da Nang and walking Hoi An's Ancient Town without wilting. It's the window we'd point most first-time visitors to.

June–August: still workable, just pack for it

June through August is technically still dry season, but it's hot and humid on the coast. Fine if you don't mind the heat — just plan for slower midday pace, more time in the water or air conditioning, and drink more than you think you need.

⚠ October–November: Hoi An's real flood risk

Hoi An's Ancient Town regularly floods in this window — it's a recurring seasonal pattern locals and hoteliers plan around, not a rare-disaster caveat. River water has reached several blocks up from the riverside in recent seasons, affecting ground-floor hotel rooms first. If your dates fall in October or November, book accommodation on higher ground, keep the rest of your itinerary flexible, and accept that the riverside streets could be underwater for a day or two.

September and December sit either side of that worst stretch — still wet-season months with rising storm risk through the back half of the year, but without October–November's specific, well-documented flood pattern.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time to visit Da Nang and Hoi An?

February–May — the region's dry season, before peak heat and humidity set in, and reliable for both beach days in Da Nang and walking Hoi An's Ancient Town.

Is summer a bad time to visit Da Nang and Hoi An?

Not bad, but hot: June–August is still technically dry season, just properly hot and humid on the coast. Workable if you pack for the heat and pace your days around it.

Does Hoi An really flood?

Yes, most years, mainly in October–November. It's a well-documented seasonal pattern — river water has reached several blocks up from the riverside in recent seasons, affecting ground-floor rooms first. Traveling then, book on higher ground and keep your plans flexible.
