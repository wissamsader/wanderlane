Best Time to Visit Beirut

April–June and September–October are the sweet spot — plus the honest trade-offs of summer's diaspora crowds, a milder-than-you'd-think winter, and what to check before you book.

Beirut doesn't have a true off-season the way colder cities do — it has a crowd-and-price season. The short version: April–June and September–October are the sweet spot, and the reason why has less to do with rain than with who else is in town.

The short answer

Lebanon is often described as the one Levant country with genuine four-season variation — a mild Mediterranean coast within an hour of snow in the mountains. For a Beirut-only trip, the two shoulder windows give you the most comfortable weather without summer's crowds or its prices.

SeasonMonthsWhat it's like
The sweet spotApril–June, Sept–OctWarm, dry and comfortable, roughly 15–28°C — good for both the city and day trips north.
Peak summerJuly–AugustHot on the coast, and the busiest, priciest season of the year — see below.
WinterDec–FebMild by regional standards (~14°C average in January), with occasional rain, wind and storms — also the cheapest hotel season, alongside November and March.

If you only take one thing from this page: no strong preference either way → go April–June or September–October.

Why summer isn't the obvious pick

☼ It's not really about the heat

July and August are hot on the coast, but the bigger factor is the Lebanese diaspora coming home for summer — a genuine annual migration that fills hotels, packs restaurants and pushes prices up across the city. If you're set on a summer trip, book your stay and any table you care about well ahead, and expect real waits at the places in our food guide.

Winter: milder than people expect, and the cheapest

Winter in Beirut is genuinely mild by regional standards — January averages around 14°C — but it does bring occasional rain, wind and storms off the Mediterranean, so it's not beach weather. In exchange, it's the cheapest hotel season of the year, alongside the shoulder months of November and March, and the city is at its quietest.

Before you pick your dates

⚠ Before you go

Lebanon currently carries formal government travel advisories, and they're worth taking seriously — but they aren't uniform. Several governments currently draw a real distinction between Beirut and Mount Lebanon, generally urging caution there, and the south and border areas, which carry stronger warnings; a cessation of hostilities announced in spring 2026 has held, though both the US and UK describe the situation as fluid. Advisories are updated often and differ by country, so treat this paragraph as a starting point, not the final word. Check your own government's current, dated advisory — travel.state.gov for US citizens, gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice for UK citizens — before you book, and again closer to your trip.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time to visit Beirut?

April–June or September–October — warm, dry and comfortable (roughly 15–28°C), without the price spikes and crowds of the July–August diaspora summer season.

Is summer a bad time to visit Beirut?

Not bad, but a trade-off: it's hot on the coast, and the busiest, priciest season of the year, as the Lebanese diaspora returns home for summer and fills hotels and restaurants. Book well ahead if you're set on visiting then.

Is Beirut safe to visit right now?

It depends on your own government's current advisory, which is exactly why we won't give you a flat yes or no here. Several governments currently distinguish between Beirut and Mount Lebanon — generally urging caution rather than an outright ban — and the country's south and border areas, which carry stronger warnings. Check the current, dated advisory at travel.state.gov or gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice before you book, and again closer to your trip.
