Best Time to Visit Berlin

May through September is the sweet spot — the honest trade-offs of peak summer, genuinely cold winters, and the one September weekend that quietly books out the whole city.

Berlin has an oceanic-to-continental climate, which is a polite way of saying: warm-ish summers, genuinely cold winters, and no month you should actively avoid. The short version — May through September, with the shoulder ends of that window beating the middle of it.

The short answer

SeasonMonthsWhat it's like
The sweet spotMay, September, early OctoberMild weather close to summer's, with noticeably thinner crowds than the July–August peak.
Peak summerJune–AugustWarmest weather of the year — highs 22–25°C, lows 12–14°C — but the busiest season, and sometimes humid.

If you only take one thing from this page: no strong preference either way → aim for May, September or early October.

Peak summer, honestly

June through August is when Berlin looks like the postcards — long daylight, terraces full, Tiergarten packed with picnics. It's also objectively the warmest window (highs of 22–25°C) and the one where the big sights and Sonnenallee's food crawl are at their most crowded. Humidity can build in the height of it, which isn't something every visitor expects from a German summer.

Why the shoulder months win

May, September and early October give you weather close enough to summer's to sit outside comfortably, without the thickest crowds at the Reichstag, Museum Island or the Brandenburg Gate. It's the same trade-off that shows up across most of the well-known European capitals, and Berlin is no exception to it.

The date that quietly fills the city

⚠ The Berlin Marathon, late September

One of the world's biggest marathons runs through the city in late September — right inside the shoulder-season sweet spot — and it fills hotels citywide. If you're not running it, check the date before you book, or you may find the 'quiet' month isn't quiet where you're staying.

Winter: cold, but not pointless

December through February runs genuinely cold — highs near 3°C, lows down to −2°C, and snow is common from December into March. What you get in exchange: the thinnest crowds of the year, and the strongest case for a specific trip — Christmas markets in late November and December, which bring their own demand and worth booking ahead for despite the weather.

☼ No month to actively dodge

Rain falls fairly evenly across the year in Berlin (roughly 570mm annually) rather than clustering in one soggy season, so there's no single month worth ruling out on rainfall alone — the real trade-offs here are temperature and crowd size, not washout risk.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best month to visit Berlin?

May, September or early October — mild weather close to summer's highs, but with noticeably thinner crowds than the June–August peak season.

Is summer a bad time to visit Berlin?

Not bad, but a trade-off: it's the warmest window (highs 22–25°C) and sometimes humid, it's the busiest tourist season at the major sights, and late September's Berlin Marathon — right at the edge of it — fills hotels citywide.

Is Berlin worth visiting in winter?

Yes, especially for the Christmas markets in late November and December, but temperatures run genuinely cold — highs around 3°C, lows down to −2°C, and snow is common from December into March.
